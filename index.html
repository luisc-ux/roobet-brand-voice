01

The voice

Five principles, two tone modes

Five writing decisions. Each says what to do and what not to do.

SHORT, not padded

Lead with the point. If it can be one sentence, make it one.

NotDon't forget, you have funds waiting in your account!YesYour balance is just sitting there. UCL kicks off in an hour.

LOUD, not yelling

Energy comes from what you say, not from !! or caps. One exclamation point per piece, max.

NotDON'T MISS OUT!! Huge rewards are waiting RIGHT NOW!!!Yes$30,000. Three games. The leaderboard closes Sunday.

SHARP, not smug

The reader is always in on the joke. Not playing never feels like failing.

NotOnly smart players are taking advantage of this.YesZeus built a roulette table. It's unhinged.

REAL, not performative

No forced slang, no trend-chasing. Copy travels, so no single-market references.

NotNo cap bestie, this promo is absolutely fire fr frYesCrash just hit 1000x. Someone was in it.

BOLD, not reckless

We hype the win and never mock the miss. Responsible language always.

NotLosers don't take chances. Winners do.YesOne winner goes to Monaco. The leaderboard is open.

ON THE EDGE

We can live close to the line. The test: does the line still work without the wink? Yes, keep it. Only works as innuendo, cut it. Never aimed at the player.

Where the edge works, and where it doesn't
WorksCRM body copy and onsite inbox (the player is already in). Sports and game campaigns (competition carries it).
CarefulSubject lines: only if the rest of the email makes it land.
Not hereWelcome emails and ACQ (too early). Anything that could read as punching at the player.
Two tone modes

CONTROLLED PLAYFUL

Promos, new games, network promotions

"Zeus built a roulette table. It's unhinged."

"Crash just hit 1000x. Someone was in it."

"Four ways to play. Dare to play them all."

QUIET CONFIDENCE

Onboarding, retention, reward confirmation, bespoke VIP gifts

"It's already in your account. Use it."

"Your balance is ready. The match isn't."

"Your account's been quiet. We put something in it."

Departments and journey stages decide which mode, and how loud. Never both at once.

02

Departments

Casino, Sportsbook, VIP, ACQ, Legal

The same voice, dialed per department. Casino is the baseline. The list can grow and each definition can be refined.

Casino · BASELINE

Full volume. The reference point for every other department.

Sharp, quick, witty, a little cheeky. Game-themed jokes and wordplay land here more naturally than anywhere else. Confident without smugness: hypes the win, never mocks the miss.

  • Length and mechanic detail depend on the piece, not the department. Casino can be short and personal too.
"Minted Mike is ready to ink the city."Personifies the game
"Watch the plane collect the multipliers."Aviamasters, framed as a literal flight

Sportsbook

Casino's precision, aimed at the match. Playful but controlled.
Reward-forwardLead with what the player gets, even when the mechanic is about a loss. "Doesn't have to cost you your bet", never "if you lose".
Mechanic precisionReal money sits on literal rules (minute 86, a 1X2 market, odds thresholds). Spell them out plainly and exactly.
"One goal in the final minutes doesn't have to cost you your bet."Late Goal Insurance

VIP

More personal, fewer emojis, never mass-market in tone.
Visual restraintFewer emojis, no doubled exclamation points.
Economy of languageShorter. Trust the reward to carry it.
Elevated vocabularyWarm, without Casino's internet slang.
Personal framingReads like it's from someone who knows you.
Two modes
  • Structured promos (leaderboards, tiers, opt-in): full Casino energy, with VIP status called out clearly.
  • Bespoke gifts: curiosity-driven, minimal, no mechanics. This is where Quiet Confidence lives.
Status can be named when it helps ("as one of our VIPs"), but the four traits carry the feeling, not the label.

ACQ

Casino, softened for someone who may not know Roobet yet.
Player-facingSecond person, warm, accessible, no platform knowledge assumed. Slogan plus a specific promise, never slogan alone.
Affiliate contentThird person ("players can..."). Describes, doesn't sell. Mechanics woven into each promo, closings that inform rather than pitch.
"Players can back the tournament winner before the competition starts and get covered if their pick finishes in the top three."Affiliate content: factual, describes the mechanic clearly

Legal

Accuracy over voice, on purpose.

This isn't "personality off". The job is accuracy: every term checked against current legal and regulatory English before it is translated. No creative liberties, no persuasive framing, no innuendo. Precision is what makes it on-brand here, the way wit does for Casino.

03

Player journey

New, Active, VIP, Lapsed, Churned

The second dial. Where a player is in their journey shapes how loud and how familiar we sound, in any department.

DepartmentHow do we write this? What we say.
Journey stageWho is this player? How loud, how familiar.

New

First days on the platform.
EnergyModerate, welcoming
FamiliarityZero assumed. Help them find their footing.
AvoidInsider slang, front-loaded mechanics
New to Roobet, or new to casinos? Many new players already know the world from other platforms. Default: they know casino basics but not Roobet, so guide them around the platform without over-explaining the games. If the brief says first-time players, step back further.

Active

Regular players.
EnergyFull department voice
FamiliarityReturning, comfortable. Lead with the prize or the moment, skip the intro.
AvoidRe-explaining the basics

VIP

High-value players. This is the player stage, not the department.
EnergyRestrained, personal. Premium feel.
FamiliarityDeep. They expect to be recognized.
AvoidGeneric segment language, mass-market tone
VIP means two things. The VIP department is a voice. The VIP stage is a player. They overlap (a VIP player still bets on sports), so briefs and copy always name which one is meant.

Lapsed

Players who've gone quiet.
EnergyQuieter, direct
FamiliarityAcknowledge the gap without making it awkward. Lead with what's waiting for them.
AvoidGuilt-tripping, vague "we miss you"

Churned

Players who've left.
EnergyLow-key, careful
FamiliarityRespect the exit. Don't guess why they left. One specific reason to come back.
AvoidPressure, over-selling the return
Combining the dials: a VIP-department piece for a New player stays quietly confident but guided, without assuming VIP history. A Casino piece for a New player and one for a Lapsed player should never read the same.

Limits and rules for every asset

Constraints are the brief. The email teases, the landing page carries the full detail.

Subject line

About 50 characters on mobile.
  • Hook in the first 4 to 6 words. Lead with the tension, the moment or the game, never the campaign name.
  • No exclamation point. One emoji maximum (testing showed no deliverability issue with one).
  • No dollar amounts, % figures or filter-trigger words. See Deliverability.
  • Username token is welcome, but it sits next to a real hook, never replaces one.

Preheader

About 85 characters. Read alongside the subject line.
  • The second beat: the detail that earns the hook. Never repeat the subject line.
  • Intrigue or a punchline. The "!" can live here, if anywhere.
  • No figures from the subject line moved here. See Deliverability.
GenericClick here to find out more about this amazing offer!
RoobetBack your pick before kickoff.
GenericHuge rewards are waiting for you. Don't miss out!
RoobetWild multipliers. Lucky Numbers. A drop that pops off.

Email body

Tease the promo, give the player a reason to click.
  • No headline in the body. Subject line and preheader are the opening.
  • Greeting on its own line: short, says something, username at the end.
  • Body: 2 to 3 short lines. Reward hook first, mechanics once. How to Play follows the first body.
  • CTA: 3 words max, 2 is better. Say what's behind the click.
  • Amounts are fine in the body, written in full ($250,000, never $250K). Dates: Month D (November 18).
  • Don't stack prize figures, opt-in language and "Promotions Page" in the opening lines.
GenericHey #username#, we have something special for you!
RoobetIt's Halloween, #user_details-username#!

Onsite inbox

The image is the click. The copy builds desire.
  • Title only, one line, no subtitle. Promo name if it fits.
  • Body up to 180 characters. One thought, one action.
  • Confirm with the stakeholder up front: desire only, or include a mechanic summary?

The rules

Format, structure, words to avoid

What applies to every piece, in every department. Open what you need.

FormatNo em dashes. One emoji max. One "!" per piece. Amounts in full ($250,000). Dates as Month D.
StructureReward first: "Get X if Y", never "If Y, get X". Reward-framed, never loss-framed.
WordingSay "Free Bet amount", never "stake". Promo names stay in English.
CTAs and fixed lines2 to 3 words, strong verb first (Play Now, Claim Reward, Join In, Keep Going, Hop In). Never "Click here". The "Don't Like Fun?" unsubscribe line never changes. Welcome emails: "Crypto's Fastest Growing Casino" plus a specific promise.
FeedbackThe brief wins on facts. Direction gets interpreted: "more exciting" means more specific, not louder.
Words and phrases to avoid EN
Don't miss outBanned. Be specific about what they'd be missing.
Huge rewards await · This is your chanceVague and generic. Use the actual number or the actual moment.
Amazing · incredible · seamlessHollow adjectives. Replace with a specific fact.
X is here · underway · heating up · in full swingAnnouncement energy. Find original phrasing.
Up for grabs · your share of · on the lineTired prize language. Say what the player gets.
ChaseNot permitted, in CTAs or anywhere else.
StakeAvoid. Use "Free Bet amount".
Locked (bonus)Internal term. Just say bonus.
Log in (to logged-in users)They're already in.
We miss youCorporate-warm. Try "Your account's been quiet. We put something in it."
Exclusive offer · limited time · click hereProve it or be specific: who it's for, "closes Sunday", what the click does.
WIN BIG · FREE!!! · GUARANTEEDSpam triggers.
Today · now · starts nowNot banned, but sparingly. They read generic.
Patterns to watch for any draft
  • Multiple exclamation points or all-caps energy.
  • Longer and vaguer than the brief.
  • Could have been written by any casino.
  • Structure flipped: "If Y, get X".
  • Negative framing up front. Eliminations, game cuts and "X players left" never open copy.
  • Recycled openers, subject structures or urgency devices across rounds of a long campaign.
  • Prize amounts or dates that don't match across brief, landing page, email and onsite.

07

Brands that get it

Calibration, not imitation

Not brands we copy. Brands that calibrate the taste. When in doubt: does this feel more like them, or like everyone else?

Wendy's

Self-aware · dry · never tries too hard

We borrow: a point of view, and a dry delivery that lands the joke without winking at the camera.

Ryanair

Anti-corporate · brutally honest · owns the joke

We borrow: the refusal to over-explain or over-apologize, and the trust that the audience gets it.

Duolingo

Consistent voice · purposeful chaos · never breaks character

We borrow: consistency. Being memorable is a long game, built one on-voice interaction at a time.

None of them sound like a casino. iGaming defaults to hype, caps and hollow urgency. Being unmistakably Roobet is the edge.

08

For writers

Pre-submit check and AI check

Optional. Only for the people producing copy. The doc templates carry the detailed reminders.

Pre-submit check 6 questions
  1. Could a competitor have written this? If yes, it isn't Roobet yet.
  2. Does it lead with the reward ("Get X if Y")?
  3. Is the tone right for the department and the player's journey stage?
  4. Do amounts and dates match across brief, landing page, email and onsite?
  5. Are channel limits respected in every language?
  6. Read it out loud. Does it sound live, not like an announcement?
Anything missing or ambiguous in the brief (dates, mechanics, game names, onsite desire-only or mechanic summary) goes to your CRM contact before writing, not after.
AI check optional

AI is a good starting point. Left unedited, it reads like AI. If more than two of these are true, rewrite.

Em dashesMultiple "!"Longer than the briefHollow adjectives"If Y, get X"Could be any casino
Use AI forFirst-draft structure, rough angles, options to react to, localization first pass, bullet logic, adapting approved EN copy.
Never let AIWrite the final subject line, make tone calls, write VIP communications, or ship copy no human has read.

Second opinions: Originality.ai, Copyleaks. And your own ear. AI writes a draft. You write Roobet.

Localizations

Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese

Same voice, different language. Open each section when you need it.

General rules all languages
Localize, don't translateSound like Roobet in that language, not a word-for-word render of English.
Limits are fixedLanguages run longer. Tighten the message, not the rules.
Exclamation pointsEN: one max. ES: even more restrained. Never fake energy with punctuation.
CTAsConjugated imperative, 2 to 3 words max. "Juega ahora", not "Jugar ahora".
Promo namesStay in English, in italics, highlighted yellow in the source so CRM can link. Mr. Roo is always Mr. Roo.
No regional lock-inUnless targeting one market, copy must work across all markets sharing that language.
CapitalizationES and PT-BR: no title case in subject lines.
esportsAlways lowercase e, in every language.
Spanish Mexico · Argentina · LATAM
RegisterNeutral tuteo, nothing regional. No voseo: agarras not agarrá. Day to day: tú, plural ustedes. For T&Cs, confirm with the requester if usted is needed.
ExclamationsEven more restrained than English. Full inverted sets: ¡Así se juega!
Numbers and dates$30,000 not $30.000, consistent with English. Dates DD/MM.
Prize pool"$X en premios" for Roobet-owned campaigns. "Pozo total" may appear in network promotions. Stay consistent within a campaign.
False friends"Billion" = "mil millones". "Billón" = trillion. Always double-check large numbers.
TerminologyFree Bets: Apuesta Gratis / Apuestas Gratis. Opt-in: Apúntate. How to Enter: Cómo unirte. Autobet: Autoapuesta. Leaderboard: ranking or tabla de clasificación.
AvoidContendientes doesn't land naturally in LATAM markets.
Keep in EnglishSportsbook, lobby, game names, client seed, stop on loss, stop on profit, jackpot tier names (Mini/Minor/Major/Mega), free chips.

Portuguese Brazil
LengthRuns longer than English, so limits matter even more. Never word-for-word.
Registervocê for day to day, not European tu. "A gente" over "nós" for natural conversation.
False cognates"Atualmente" is not "actually". The English source should say "currently" or "nowadays".
Numbers and datesThousands with a dot ($1.000), decimals with a comma (1,7). Dates DD/MM. Ranges: "28/08 a 10/09".
Japanese Japan
S-L-A-PSpeak Like A Person. No nominalizing verbs, no repetition, write succinctly. Space after question marks.
NewslettersSpoken, conversational written form, not formal document language.
Word orderDon't mirror English structure. Translate context, not words.
Numbers and datesDate AA/MM/DD. Alphanumeric characters for numbers. Currency: $.
